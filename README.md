## Fees: where MEXC's zero actually matters

MEXC's headline is real. Standard spot pricing on the platform is 0% maker and 0.05% taker, and that applies from your first trade with no volume minimum and no token requirement. Gate's entry tier is 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker, which is the standard rate most big exchanges charge at VIP 0.

Run it on a number rather than a percentage. $50,000 of monthly spot volume is $600,000 a year. If all of it rests on the book, Gate's VIP 0 maker rate bills $600 and MEXC bills $0. If all of it crosses the spread, MEXC's 0.05% taker comes to $300 a year, and Gate's 0.10% comes to $600 — or $540 if you pay fees in GT.

That gap narrows fast once you look at how each platform discounts. MEXC has two mechanisms that don't stack: holding 500 or more MX in your spot account for 24 consecutive hours cuts spot and futures fees by 50%, taking the taker rate to 0.025%, while enabling MX deduction takes 20% off, landing at 0.04%. MEXC takes three random snapshots a day and the lowest balance decides, so parking MX for an afternoon doesn't count. Gate's GT token works differently — GT holdings count toward your VIP tier and paying fees in GT is cheaper than the standard rate, but only from VIP 0 through VIP 9. From VIP 10 up, the standard and GT columns are the same number.

Two more quirks worth knowing before you plan around either schedule:

- Gate makers get no discount at all from VIP 0 through VIP 3 — maker and taker are the same rate until VIP 4. If you're a patient limit-order trader, you're paying the taker rate for four tiers.
- Gate's Alpha trading fee is a flat 0.8% at every tier, with no VIP relief.

On perpetuals, MEXC publishes a standard rate of 0.01% maker and 0.04% taker, with special contract pricing as low as 0% maker and 0.02% taker on BTCUSDT. Gate's VIP 0 perpetual rate is roughly 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker. Per-contract terms vary on both venues, so the fee page beats any summary table, including this one.

The caveat that catches people running bots: API-routed futures orders on MEXC are priced at 0.06% maker and 0.08% taker, well above its web rates, and neither MX deductions nor fee promotions apply to them. If you're automating futures, model that number, not the 0%.

## How many coins does each one really list?

This is where almost every comparison quietly cheats. Gate's own marketing says more than 4,900 assets; MEXC says over 3,000. Third-party trackers count much lower, because they only count what they've indexed. CoinGecko was tracking roughly 1,676 MEXC spot coins across 2,054 pairs and about 1,562 Gate coins across 1,705 pairs in one August 2026 snapshot.

Platform numbers and tracker numbers measure different things, so comparing them is pointless. The honest conclusion is that neither exchange wins the catalogue question by a margin worth arguing about — Gate's self-reported figure includes assets across products and isn't a spot-pair count.

The real difference is listing speed. MEXC published 135 new spot listings in March 2026 with 65% of them platform-first, and CoinGecko counted 879 new MEXC perpetuals between January 2025 and April 2026. Gate doesn't publish a periodic listing count, so its pipeline can't be checked from outside the company. If being early to a token is your edge, that asymmetry matters more than either catalogue total.

Both venues carry the same warning label, though. A long list of small-cap pairs mostly means most pairs are thin. CoinGecko rated MEXC's liquidity as moderate with an average bid-ask spread around 0.98%, and broad-spread pairs quietly erase the 0% maker advantage — the fee is zero but the spread isn't. If you trade illiquid tokens in size, measure the spread before you measure the fee.

## Licences, KYC and where you can actually use each platform

This is the cleanest dividing line between the two, and it's not close.

Gate holds a MiCA authorisation passported across the EEA, a Japan FSA registration, a Dubai VARA licence and an AUSTRAC registration. MEXC holds none of those. MEXC isn't authorised under MiCA and appeared on ESMA's register of non-compliant entities after a September 2025 decision by the Netherlands Authority for the Financial Markets, advised EU users to complete withdrawals before 1 July, appears on Japan's FSA list of unregistered operators, and was added to the UK FCA's warning list in March 2024.

MEXC also doesn't serve residents of the United States or the United Kingdom, and Gate's global platform isn't offered to US residents either — those users get a separate Gate US entity with a far shorter asset list and its own licensing path.

If you're in the EEA or Japan, the fee comparison should not be the deciding factor. A 0.05% saving on taker fees is worth very little against the risk of a platform losing access to your market mid-position, which is exactly what MEXC's EU users were told to prepare for.

On verification, the two take different approaches to onboarding. Gate pushes identity verification early in the process, before you're moving meaningful money. MEXC has historically let people trade before completing KYC with limits on what they can withdraw. Whichever you pick, do the verification early anyway — it's what unlocks withdrawal limits, and it's what the reward programmes are conditioned on.

## FAQ

**Is MEXC cheaper than Gate?**
On published entry rates, yes — 0% maker and 0.05% taker on spot versus Gate's 0.10% both ways at VIP 0. MEXC's advantage is real for anyone placing limit orders, and it applies from the first trade with no conditions attached. It shrinks or disappears if you trade illiquid pairs where the spread is wider than the fee difference.

**Which exchange has more coins?**
Neither by a defensible margin. Gate self-reports the larger figure and CoinGecko tracks a similar number on both, with MEXC ahead on tracker counts in recent snapshots. The meaningful difference is that MEXC publishes its listing pace and Gate doesn't, so MEXC's pipeline is verifiable and Gate's isn't.

**Can I use both?**
Yes, and plenty of people do — one venue for low-fee altcoin rotation, another for Earn products, stocks or a licensed on-ramp in a regulated market. Registration is free on both. If you're running accounts in two places, keep only what you're actively trading on each and move the rest to a wallet you control.

**Which one is safer?**
They're not equivalent on this. Gate holds licences in the EEA, Japan, Dubai and Australia and publishes monthly proof-of-reserves reports; MEXC holds none of those licences and has been flagged by regulators in the EU, UK and Japan. Both hold the bulk of funds in cold storage and offer 2FA. If regulatory standing matters to you, that's Gate.

**Does Gate require KYC?**
Verification is part of its onboarding flow and unlocks full trading and withdrawal limits. MEXC has historically allowed limited trading before verification. Either way, verify — it's a condition of nearly every reward tier on both platforms.
